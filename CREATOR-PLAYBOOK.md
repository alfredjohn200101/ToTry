# CREATOR PLAYBOOK — documenting the journey without performing it

> For Alfy. An honest, humane guide to recording your own transformation and building something bigger —
> grounded in the evidence, and in the same soul you built into the app. Research: round-10 subagent.

## The one north star
**Document the living; don't live to document.** The version of this that *grows* a person and the version
that *performs* one look identical from outside but are opposite in mechanism. The whole game is staying on the
right side of that line. **If recording is ever degrading the life, the life wins — put the camera down.**

## The most important science — report reps, not resolutions
Your instinct ("announce my transformation") points straight at a real, proven trap:
- **The sabotage (Gollwitzer, *When Intentions Go Public*, 2009):** announcing an *identity goal* to people who
  notice it gives your brain a premature "sense of completeness" — it mistakes the *talking* for the *doing* and
  you invest **less** effort. It held even among the strongly committed. (Sivers' version: announcers quit after
  ~33 min feeling close; the quiet group worked the full 45.)
- **The help:** public commitment *does* aid follow-through — but only when it's a **concrete action (not an
  identity claim)**, has a **real cost of failure**, and involves **ongoing check-in**.
- **The reconciliation (and it fits video perfectly): don't broadcast the outcome or the identity — broadcast the
  process and the finished work.**
  - ✗ "I'm going to become a new, disciplined man of faith." (identity goal → premature completeness → effort drops)
  - ✓ "Here's what I actually *did* today / this week." (finished reps = evidence, can't hand you a false finish)
  - **Rule: past tense over future tense.** "I trained, I prayed, I read this, here's what I learned." Let the
    identity be the conclusion others *draw* — never the claim you *make*.

## Scars, not wounds (protect yourself)
- Share from the **healed scar** (a survived, processed insight), never the **open wound** (processing live, in
  public). Raw disclosure risks re-opening it in you *and* transferring the pain to the people you're trying to help.
- **Readiness test:** *Can you receive a cruel or careless comment about this without spiraling?* If not, it's not
  ready to publish yet — journal it privately instead.
- Brené Brown: "Vulnerability minus boundaries is not vulnerability." Share from your heart, not your hurt.
- **Plan for the lapse NOW.** Recovery on a public clock is fragile — a slip becomes a *public* slip. Decide in
  advance it gets framed as feedback, not failure (grace over shame — the same rule as the app), and that the
  living comes before the reporting.

## The cadence — capture daily (private), publish weekly
- **Daily (private, low-stakes):** a 60-second voice note or selfie-video *journal* — NOT for publishing. Three
  prompts: **What did I actually do? What did I feel? What's one thing I learned?** Most never see daylight —
  they're the raw ore, and they keep the habit without the pressure (or the premature-completeness trap) of performing daily.
- **Weekly (the anchor):** one intentional video. One genuinely good video a week beats daily mediocre uploads —
  consistency + a clear system beats raw frequency. **Batch** it from the week's captures in one focused session.
  Build in a recovery rhythm so the schedule never becomes the thing that breaks you.

## The weekly video arc (~2–4 min)
1. **Hook** — one real, specific moment from the week (not a thesis statement).
2. **Where I actually am** — honest context, including the messy middle.
3. **What happened** — the reps, *including what didn't work* (this is the trust-builder).
4. **The turn** — the one insight/scar you can now share from a healed place.
5. **The invitation** — small, pointing *beyond yourself* (a question, a book, an action *they* can take) — never "download my app."

## Topic pillars — so it's a life, not an ad (the app is 1 of 5)
1. **Your own reps** — discipline, training, the daily practice. Shown, not preached.
2. **Faith** — honest and lived, wrestling included; full but never forced (the app's own rule).
3. **Ideas you're learning** — books, thinkers, one idea at a time. Teaching what you're learning is the most
   durable, least self-exposing content there is — and it makes you a *student*, not a guru.
4. **People & service** — relationships, showing up for others, the human moments (with their consent).
5. **The build** — the app and its *why*, as a **quarter** of the story, not the whole.

## Lead with WHY, win the few
- **Start with why** (Sinek): people connect to *why*, not *what*. Your why — grace over shame, presence not
  performance, points beyond itself — is the differentiator no competitor can copy. Start there; touch the app only sometimes.
- **1,000 True Fans** (Kevin Kelly): you don't need millions, you need a thousand who genuinely care — won by
  giving value freely over time. This is also the healthier target for your own mind than chasing view-counts.

## AVOID (name these out loud)
- **Broadcasting the identity/outcome** ("I'm going to become a new man") — the Gollwitzer trap. Report what you *did*.
- **Hustle/grind-glorification** ("no days off") — contradicts grace-over-shame, leads to burnout.
- **Strategic / manufactured vulnerability** — deployed as a growth tactic; audiences detect it and it hollows out the real thing.
- **Performing or commodifying pain** (yours or others') — scars, never open wounds.
- **Over-promising transformation** ("90 days to a new life") — sets up public failure and shame.
- **Parasocial dependence** — an audience that needs *you*, or a self that needs the audience. Point beyond
  yourself; keep a firewall — **the man is not the channel** (78% of creators report burnout, most from identity fusion).
- **Metrics obsession** — self-worth tied to views/followers. Track depth (true fans), consider a periodic "numbers fast."
- **Letting documentation eclipse living** — the master rule.

**North star, one line:** *Document the living; don't live to document. Report reps, not resolutions. Share
scars, not wounds. Keep a thousand real relationships, not a million shallow ones. And let every piece point
past yourself — the same soul you built into the app.*
