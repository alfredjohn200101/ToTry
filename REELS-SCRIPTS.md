## TO TRY — 12 SHOOTABLE SCRIPTS

Companion to `CREATOR-PLAYBOOK.md`. That file governs *why* and *when* you publish; this one is the *what to point the camera at*. Same rules apply here: past tense, reps not resolutions, scars not wounds, and every video useful even to someone who never installs anything.

**Before you shoot anything (read once, then forget it):**
- iPhone screen record: Settings → Control Centre → add Screen Recording. Long-press the button → mic ON if you're talking over it, OFF for the silent ones.
- Turn on Do Not Disturb first. A notification banner mid-take kills the take.
- Talking-head bits: phone against a wall at chest height, one window to your side, stand 60cm back. That's the whole lighting rig.
- Record voiceover separately in Voice Memos while walking around your room. You'll sound like a person instead of a presenter.
- Do the screen actions **slowly** — about 30% slower than you'd naturally tap. Viewers can't follow your thumb.
- One take is allowed to be the take. Being a beginner on camera is the trust; polish is what people distrust.
- Every script below is written so nothing on screen is invented — these are the real labels, real copy, real buttons in v367.

## 1 · THE ONE BUTTON
**Format: silent screen-record, text only (no voice at all)**

**HOOK (0–2s)**
Spoken: *nothing.* This one is silent on purpose.
On-screen text: **"I put one button on every screen. It isn't a menu."**
Visual: home screen, thumb resting near the small gold-to-purple orb bottom-right. It's breathing — a slow halo in and out. Let it pulse twice before anything happens.

**THE PROBLEM**
It's 11pm, you feel something you can't name, and your thumb is already on Instagram before you've noticed you're upset.

**SCREEN ACTIONS**
- 0–3s — Home. Hero + the "Your life, woven" card behind. Orb breathing bottom-right.
- 3–6s — Tap the orb. Sheet slides up: **"What are you feeling, Alfy?"**
- 6–12s — Slow scroll so all ten chips read: The pull · Restless · Flat · Anxious · Heavy · Heartache · Avoiding · Can't start · Fired up · Actually good. Pause a half-beat on "Can't start — frozen, staring at it".
- 12–17s — Tap **Restless — can't settle, antsy**. Card: **"Restless energy"** — *"That buzz wants OUT, not in. Don't scroll it away — that just traps it."*
- 17–23s — Pan down the options so they read: **Log a quick workout** · **🫁 Take a breath first** · **💬 Just talk** / **✎ Write it down** · **🌄 Go outside and look up** · **🔒 Make my plan for next time**.
- 23–29s — Tap **🔒 Make my plan for next time**. The box is already filled in: *"burn it off — 20 push-ups or a fast walk, not the scroll."* Tap **Lock in my plan**. Toast: *"Plan locked in."*
- 29–34s — Back out, tap **Restless** again. Now a gold panel sits at the top of the card: **"The plan you set for this"** — with his own sentence in it.

**VOICEOVER**
None. Silence is the point of this one.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT**
1. "I put one button on every screen. It isn't a menu."
2. "It asks one question."
3. "Ten answers. No typing."
4. "It doesn't log the feeling. It moves you out of it."
5. "And you can write the plan while you're calm…"
6. "…so next time, it hands you back your own words."

**THE TURN**
At 29s, when his own sentence comes back to him. Every other app would have saved the *feeling* and shown him a chart of it next month. This saved the *decision* and gave it back at the exact moment he'd have forgotten it.

**CTA**
On-screen, small, last frame: *"Free. Link in bio if you want it. If you don't — write your own version on a bit of paper. Same mechanism."*

**CAPTION**
No voiceover on this one because I want you to just watch the thing. The whole app has one button on every screen and it asks what you're feeling, not what you did. Ten answers, no typing — because nobody types when they're in it. Write your plan while you're clear-headed and it reads it back to you when you're not. That's it. That's the trick.

## 2 · THE APP THAT TELLS YOU TO PUT IT DOWN
**Format: problem-then-demo**

**HOOK (0–2s)**
Spoken: *"Every app on your phone is built so you don't leave. I built the ending in on purpose."*
On-screen text: **"THE ONLY SCREEN I'M PROUD OF"**
Visual: you, close, phone in hand, screen facing away from camera.

**THE PROBLEM**
A phone has no last page — no credits, no bottom — so you never actually finish. You just eventually stop.

**SCREEN ACTIONS**
- 0–4s — Talking head, one line.
- 4–8s — Cut to screen. Fight tab. Sobriety clock at the top, big number, **"Days clean"**, seconds ticking underneath.
- 8–14s — Tap the vice card → **"You came here first."** Rows appear: *🌿 N days clean. This is the moment that keeps it, or ends it.* · *💰 $X already reclaimed by not buying.* · *🧠 What's this really about right now — tired, bored, stressed, alone?*
- 14–19s — Tap **"The wave's passing — I'm good."**
- 19–27s — Full screen goes dark green-black. 🕊️ **"You came here instead."** Then: *"Now put me down, Alfy. Go live the next hour — the real one, off the glass. I'm right here if the wave comes back. But you don't need me right now."*
- 27–32s — Tap **"I'm putting it down."** Screen: **"Go well."** — fades to nothing. Phone goes in pocket, on camera.
- 32–38s — Cut back to home. Bottom of the "Your life, woven" card: *"🕊️ 14 times, you came here and walked back into your life."*

**VOICEOVER**
"Every other app is measured by how long you stay. So I built the opposite — the only number this thing is proud of. Not time in the app. The times you came in, sorted yourself out, and left. It says it out loud: put me down, go and live the next hour. And then it actually goes away. No 'what's next.' No feed underneath. That's the whole screen."

**ON-SCREEN TEXT**
- "no last page = you never stop"
- "so I built one"
- "'Now put me down.'"
- "the only metric it keeps: times you LEFT"

**THE TURN**
"Go well" fading to a blank screen. No engagement app can ship that frame — it's revenue walking out the door. That's the proof the incentive is different, and you don't have to argue for it, you just show it.

**CTA**
"It's free and there's nothing to buy in it, so I've got no reason to keep you here. If you take one thing from this: decide in advance what the *end* of your phone session looks like. It won't happen by itself."

**CAPTION**
Adam Alter's line stuck with me — a phone has no last page, so we never put it down. So I built a last page. When you get through a hard moment in To Try it doesn't congratulate you and drop you into a feed; it tells you to put the phone down and go live the next hour, then it disappears. The only number it counts is how many times you left. Anti-engagement isn't a bug in this one.
`#dopaminedetox #screentime #mentalhealth #appdesign #buildinpublic #digitalminimalism`

## 3 · WHY I REFUSED TO PAYWALL THE SKILLS
**Format: "I built this because" — talking head, then screen**

**HOOK (0–2s)**
Spoken: *"Every app teaches you this in the middle of the crisis. Nobody teaches it to you on a Tuesday afternoon when nothing's wrong."*
On-screen text: **"learn the tool BEFORE the moment"**
Visual: you sitting, not standing. Lower energy than the other videos. This one's a conversation.

**THE PROBLEM**
The techniques that get you through 11pm are the ones you've never once practised at 3pm.

**SCREEN ACTIONS**
- 0–7s — Talking head only. Don't touch the phone yet.
- 7–11s — Screen: Fight tab, scroll to the card. **"The toolkit — Learn the tool before you need it. Eight skills, two minutes each — the same ones I use with you at 11pm. Free, and nothing to finish."** Tap **Learn the tools — 2 min each**.
- 11–18s — Scroll the eight slowly: 🌊 Riding the wave · ⛅ Thoughts as weather · 🧲 Catching the sentence before the slip · 🪞 Why one becomes five · 🫶 Why the hard voice loses · 🧭 Doing it when you don't feel like it · 👟 Action comes first · 🍽 The need under the urge.
- 18–26s — Open **"Action comes first."** Scroll to its evidence block and let the camera sit on it long enough to read: *"Behavioural activation is the strongest-evidenced thing on this list… it performs about as well as full cognitive behavioural therapy for depression."*
- 26–33s — Open **"The need under the urge"** and rest on ITS evidence line: *"Be straight about this one: HALT is a mnemonic out of recovery practice, not a tested intervention — nobody has run a trial on the acronym."*
- 33–40s — Bottom of the lesson: *"These are skills, not treatment… If what you're facing is bigger than an hour, that deserves a real person, not a better exercise."*

**VOICEOVER**
"There are eight of these. Two minutes each, free, no streak, no percentage, nothing to complete. And the bit I actually care about — every single one tells you how good the evidence for it is. This one's got the best track record on the list, and it says so. And this one — the hungry-angry-lonely-tired thing everyone quotes — it just tells you straight: that's a mnemonic somebody made up in recovery rooms, nobody's ever tested the acronym. Judge it on whether it helps you. I'm not going to overclaim a technique to somebody who's suffering. That's just a nicer way of lying to them."

**ON-SCREEN TEXT**
- "8 skills · 2 min each · free"
- "no streak. nothing to finish."
- "every lesson tells you how strong the evidence ISN'T"
- "'not a tested intervention' — in the app itself"

**THE TURN**
26–33s. Watching an app argue *against* its own feature. Nobody does that, because nobody's incentive allows it. That single screen tells a stranger more about who built it than any founder story would.

**CTA**
"If you never open my app, open the idea: go and learn one of these while you're calm. Behavioural activation. Look it up, it takes ten minutes. It's the one with the receipts."

**CAPTION**
Eight CBT and ACT skills, two minutes each, free — the same ones the app uses with you at 11pm, taught somewhere nothing is happening. The part I'd defend to anyone: each lesson carries its own honest evidence line, including the ones where the evidence is thin. HALT is a mnemonic, not a tested intervention, and the app says so. Overclaiming to someone who's suffering is just a polite way of lying to them.

## 4 · "URGES LAST 15 MINUTES" — WHERE THAT ACTUALLY COMES FROM
**Format: myth-correction**

**HOOK (0–2s)**
Spoken: *"You've heard cravings last 15 to 20 minutes. That number isn't from a study."*
On-screen text: **"15–20 MINUTES"** with a line struck through it.
Visual: you, mid-sentence, no intro. Straight in.

**THE PROBLEM**
You white-knuckle to twenty minutes, it's still there at twenty-five, and now you think you're the exception — so you fold.

**SCREEN ACTIONS**
- 0–8s — Talking head, direct to camera.
- 8–14s — Screen: Toolkit → **"Riding the wave."** Read the top: *"An urge is not a straight line that climbs until you give in. It rises, it peaks, and it comes down — whether or not you feed it."*
- 14–21s — Scroll to the evidence block and hold: *"The 'urges pass in 15–30 minutes' line you hear everywhere is a clinical rule of thumb, not a measured law — yours may be shorter or longer."*
- 21–28s — Tap **"Breathe one through now."** The Reset breath starts: orb expands, **"Breathe in"** (short), **"Sip a little more"**, **"Long slow out"**. Let two full cycles play on camera, no talking over them.
- 28–34s — It ends and asks the 0–10 after-rating. Tap a number. Cut.

**VOICEOVER**
"The shape is real — up, peak, down, whether you feed it or not. That's the whole reason you can outlast one. The *number* is a rule of thumb from clinical practice, not something anyone measured. And that matters, because if you've been told twenty minutes and you're still standing there at twenty-five, you decide you're broken. You're not. Yours is just yours. So the app doesn't sell you a stopwatch — it gives you something to do with your body while it passes. Two of these. In, sip a bit more, long slow out. That's the fastest way down there is."

**ON-SCREEN TEXT**
- "the SHAPE is real"
- "the NUMBER is a rule of thumb"
- "not a measured law"
- "if it's still there at 25 min — you're not the exception"

**THE TURN**
21s. The app hands you a mechanism instead of a countdown — and it corrected the myth the entire self-help internet repeats, including the version most quitting apps put on their home screen.

**CTA**
"Stop timing it. Next one, just do the breath — in, sip, long slow out — and stop checking the clock. Costs nothing, no app needed."

**CAPTION**
Correcting something I used to repeat: "urges last 15–20 minutes" is a clinical rule of thumb, not a finding. The shape is real — an urge rises, peaks and falls whether you feed it or not. The stopwatch isn't. And the difference matters, because when the timer runs out and it's still there, people decide they're the exception and give in. In · sip a little more · long slow out. Two of those.
`#mythbusting #cravings #recovery #urgesurfing #breathwork #mentalhealth`

- "bored. it's always bored."
- "'I'm buying it anyway — that's allowed'"

**THE TURN**
21s. The line *"Why you started"* — your own reason, pulled from a completely different part of the app, surfacing in a shop. Nothing that's only a budgeting app has that sentence to give you.

**CTA**

**CAPTION**
What I do now instead of buying things. ## 8 · TWO MINUTES, AND IT SHRINKS UNTIL YOU CAN
**Format: silent screen-record, text only (second one — but faster cuts and a real timer on screen, so it doesn't feel like #1)**

**HOOK (0–2s)**
Spoken: *nothing.*
On-screen text: **"For the thing you've been staring at for three days."**
Visual: the Feeling Door already open, thumb hovering over **"Can't start — frozen, staring at it."**

**THE PROBLEM**
It's not that you're avoiding it. You're sat right in front of it and you cannot make your hands move.

**SCREEN ACTIONS**
- 0–3s — Feeling Door. Tap **Can't start — frozen, staring at it.**
- 3–7s — Form: **"What can't you start?"** — *"Name it in a few words. Naming it is already the first inch."* Type: `the assignment`. Tap **Make it smaller**.

- 11–15s — Tap **"Still too big — make it smaller."** → *"Smaller: open it. Put the file, the page or the app on your screen and nothing else."*
- 15–19s — Tap it again → *"Smaller still: stand up and put one object where it needs to be."*
- 19–24s — Once more → *"Then just this: sit where you'd do it, for two minutes, doing nothing."* And underneath: *"If even that's too much today, that's information about your capacity, not your character. Rest counts."*
- 24–30s — Tap **Start two minutes.** Full-screen: **"You started"** and a real clock counting **2:00 → 1:59 → 1:58**. Let it actually run for four seconds on camera.
- 30–34s — Cut to the desk, laptop open, you working. Timer still visible on the phone beside it.

**VOICEOVER**
None.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT**
1. "For the thing you've been staring at for three days."
2. "name it →"
3. "too big?"
4. "smaller."
5. "smaller."
6. "smaller."
7. "'that's information about your capacity, not your character.'"
8. "2:00"
9. "(still going, 40 min later)"

**THE TURN**
19–24s. It keeps shrinking the task until it hits *"sit there and do nothing for two minutes"* — and then tells you rest counts if even that's too much. It never once decides you're lazy. Nothing that runs on streaks can afford to say that.

**CTA**
Last frame, text only: *"Free. Or just do this yourself: halve the task until it's stupid, then start."*

**CAPTION**
Being frozen isn't the same as avoiding something, and it doesn't feel like laziness from the inside — it feels like the residue of every time you tried before. So this door just makes the thing smaller. And smaller. Until it's "sit where you'd do it for two minutes, doing nothing." And if that's still too much, it says: that's information about your capacity, not your character. Rest counts. Then a visible two minutes, because two abstract minutes aren't real.

## 9 · BEFORE YOU FIGHT THE URGE — FOUR TAPS
**Format: talking-head then screen**

**HOOK (0–2s)**
Spoken: *"Half the time you're not craving anything. You just haven't eaten since one o'clock."*
On-screen text: **"H · A · L · T"**
Visual: you, kitchen, evening, actually eating something. Talk with your mouth half full if that's what happens.

**THE PROBLEM**
11pm, the pull is enormous, and you're gearing up for a battle of willpower you were never going to win in that state.

**SCREEN ACTIONS**
- 0–8s — Talking head in the kitchen.
- 8–12s — Screen: Fight tab → the vice card → **"🍽 Wait — am I hungry, angry, lonely or tired?"**
- 12–17s — **"Before anything else — what's actually going on?"** — *"An urge is often a different need wearing a disguise. Pick whichever is truest right now. More than one can be."* Four buttons: 🍽 Hungry · 🔥 Angry · 🫂 Lonely · 😴 Tired.
- 17–23s — Tap **🫂 Lonely.** Response: *"This one matters most. Isolation feeds the urge and contact starves it — reaching one real person does more here than any technique."* Button: **Reach someone.**

## SHOOTING ORDER (so you're not staring at twelve)

Batch these — one screen-record session, one talking-head session, and two you have to catch in real life.

| Shoot together | Scripts | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Session A — screen only, ~40 min** | 1, 8, 6, 10 | No face, no lighting, no nerves. Ship one of these first. |
| **Session B — talking head + screen, one sitting, same shirt is fine** | 3, 4, 5, 9 | Four hooks back-to-back, then all the screen b-roll after. |
| **Catch in the wild — no scheduling** | 7 (next time you're in a shop), 12 (any real morning + that same night) | These only work if they're actually real. Don't stage them. |
| **When you're ready, not before** | 2, 11 | 2 is the thesis video — worth being rested for. 11 is the scar one: only shoot it if a careless comment underneath wouldn't touch you. |

**Post #1 should be script 1.** Silent, no face, no voice, 34 seconds, and it shows the single most distinctive thing in the app. If it goes nowhere you've lost forty minutes and learned the format. If it lands, you have eleven more ready.

**Two rules from the playbook that apply hardest to these twelve:** every CTA above gives the mechanism away free so the video still helps someone who never installs anything — that's what stops a tutorial being an advert. And none of them claims a transformation, promises a timeline, or announces who you're becoming. They report what the thing does, in past tense, and let people draw their own conclusion.
